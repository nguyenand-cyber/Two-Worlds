Two Plans for One Country

After the American Revolution, the United States needed a plan to run the country. The first plan did not work well. In this activity, you will compare it with the plan we still use today.

1789U.S. ConstitutionThe new plan

Step 1: Read the story

A weak government on purpose

In 1776, the 13 colonies said they were free from Great Britain. They needed a plan for a new government. In 1781, the states approved the Articles of Confederation. This was the country's first plan.

The Articles kept the national government weak on purpose. Americans had just fought against a king. They did not want a strong ruler again. Most power stayed with the 13 states.

The Articles did some things right

The national government under the Articles finished the war and signed the Treaty of Paris in 1783. In 1787, it also passed the Northwest Ordinance. This law set up a way for new territories to become states.

Then the problems grew

Congress could not collect taxes. It could only ask the states for money, and many states did not pay. There was no president and no national court. Congress also had no army of its own.

In 1786 and 1787, farmers in Massachusetts were angry about debts and taxes. A former army captain named Daniel Shays helped lead them. They shut down courts so judges could not take away farms. This is called Shays' Rebellion. Congress had no money or army to help. Massachusetts raised its own militia, using money from wealthy Boston merchants.

Many leaders were shocked. They asked: "Is this government strong enough to keep the country together?"

A new plan

In the summer of 1787, delegates met in Philadelphia. They planned to fix the Articles. Instead, they wrote a brand new plan: the Constitution. It created a stronger national government with three branches. It took effect in 1789 after enough states approved it.

Not everyone agreed

Federalists supported the Constitution. They said the country needed a stronger national government to stay safe and united.

Anti-Federalists had doubts. They worried that a strong national government could take power from the states and hurt people's freedoms. Their worries led to the Bill of Rights, which was added in 1791.
